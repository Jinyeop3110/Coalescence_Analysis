## 1. Introduction - Community Coalescence Framework

### Paper 1.1: Integrative Framework for Coalescence
- **Title:** Toward an integrative framework for microbial community coalescence
- **Journal:** Trends in Microbiology (2024)
- **URL:** https://www.cell.com/trends/microbiology/abstract/S0966-842X(23)00262-7
- **Key Finding:** Proposes an integrative framework aligning community coalescence with biological invasion theory; discusses how organismal interactions and cohesive establishment affect coalescence outcomes.
- **Where to cite:** Introduction paragraph 2, alongside Rillig2015
- **Suggested sentence:** "Recent frameworks have aligned community coalescence with biological invasion theory, emphasizing the interplay between dispersal, mixing, and community reassembly [REF]."

### Paper 1.2: Drivers and Consequences of Coalescence
- **Title:** Drivers and consequences of microbial community coalescence
- **Journal:** ISME Journal (2024)
- **Authors:** Liu & Salles
- **URL:** https://academic.oup.com/ismej/article/18/1/wrae179/7759390
- **Key Finding:** Reviews the four-step coalescence process and mechanisms driving community coalescence, with emphasis on source and resident community fates.
- **Where to cite:** Introduction paragraph 2
- **Suggested sentence:** Add as additional citation with Rillig2015: "\citep{Rillig2015, Liu2024}"

### Paper 1.3: Priority Effects Review
- **Title:** Priority effects in microbiome assembly
- **Journal:** Nature Reviews Microbiology (2021)
- **URL:** https://www.nature.com/articles/s41579-021-00604-w
- **Key Finding:** Priority effects—where arrival order determines community outcomes—have been observed across diverse microbial environments and can lead to alternative stable states.
- **Where to cite:** Discussion section
- **Suggested sentence:** "Priority effects—where arrival order determines community outcomes—share mechanistic parallels with coalescence, as both reflect history-dependent dynamics shaped by interaction strength [REF]."

## 2. Introduction/Discussion - Emergent Properties & Units of Selection

### Paper 2.1: Higher-Order Interactions and Emergent Properties
- **Title:** Higher-order interactions and emergent properties of microbial communities: The power of synthetic ecology
- **Journal:** Heliyon (2024)
- **URL:** https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2405844024099274
- **Key Finding:** Emergent properties—patterns not deducible from constituent parts—underlie important ecological characteristics such as resilience, niche expansion, and spatial self-organization.
- **Where to cite:** Discussion, when discussing the emergent regime
- **Suggested sentence:** "Emergent properties—patterns not deducible linearly from the properties of constituent parts—underlie important ecological characteristics including resilience and niche expansion [REF], consistent with the unpredictability we observe in the emergent regime."

### Paper 2.2: Microbial Communities as Experimental Units
- **Title:** Microbial Communities as Experimental Units
- **Journal:** PMC (2011)
- **URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3128510/
- **Key Finding:** Microbial communities can be units of selection under specific conditions; selection may act on community-level properties simultaneously with individual organisms.
- **Where to cite:** Introduction paragraph 3 (holistic evidence)
- **Suggested sentence:** "These findings align with the broader perspective that microbial communities can function as experimental units subject to selection [REF]."

### Paper 2.3: Multilevel Selection in Microbes
- **Title:** Multilevel selection analysis of a microbial social trait
- **Journal:** Molecular Systems Biology (2013)
- **URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3779802/
- **Key Finding:** Selection can occur simultaneously at multiple levels; the balance between individual and group selection determines evolutionary fate of social traits.
- **Where to cite:** Introduction or Discussion
- **Suggested sentence:** "Multilevel selection theory predicts that group-level benefits can favor community-level traits even when individual-level selection acts against them [REF]."
